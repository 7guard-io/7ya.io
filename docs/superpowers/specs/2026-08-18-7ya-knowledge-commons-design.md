# 7YA Knowledge Commons — Design

**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Status:** Approved direction, awaiting written-spec review

## Goal

Extend the approved 7YA life-first autobiography into a public, multilingual knowledge commons: Igor Vepretski remains the first-person narrative spine, while every major life chapter opens outward into relevant schools of thought, research, practical tools, source material, organizations, and reusable public-learning resources.

The site must serve two purposes simultaneously:

1. Tell Igor's story with maximum personal depth, emotional honesty, evidence, interviews, podcasts, viral posts, music and live/current material.
2. Convert the experience behind that story into knowledge and tools that other people can use.

## Core thesis

**Life → lived moment → public expression → public echo → schools of thought → evidence → practical tools → action → return to the story.**

The site is not a monument to its subject. Igor is the narrative entry point into a larger public-learning system.

## Narrative principle

The first-person autobiography remains dominant. Every chapter begins with lived experience, not theory.

The default editorial rhythm is:

1. **What I lived** — first-person narrative, concrete and emotionally specific.
2. **What I said then** — post, interview, podcast, article, song, video or other public expression.
3. **What happened next** — reposts, reactions, media pickup, comments, interviews, institutional or social consequences.
4. **What I understand now** — present-day reflection.
5. **What different schools say** — multiple relevant frameworks, including disagreement and critique.
6. **What the evidence supports** — research, limitations, uncertainty and source quality.
7. **What you can do with this** — practical tools, organizations, exercises, references and next actions.
8. **Take it forward** — reusable citation, share link, teaching card, resource list, or project pathway.

## Tone and leadership posture

The narrative voice is intensely personal, direct, unsanitized and leadership-oriented.

- Do not reduce Igor to a CV, campaign profile or sanitized success story.
- Do not turn pain into spectacle.
- Do show failures, contradictions, financial pressure, institutional friction, fatherhood, ambition, exhaustion, recovery, creative work and public responsibility where publication is safe and appropriate.
- Leadership framing must come from responsibility and execution: pain → understanding → responsibility → building → opportunity for others.
- The political/public-action voice should be assertive and clear, but not unsupported, triumphalist, or falsely authoritative.

## Privacy boundary

Deep personal storytelling does not authorize the exposure of other people's private lives.

Three publication classes apply:

- **PUBLIC:** already public, user-approved, source-backed, or safe first-person reflection.
- **PERSONAL-SAFE:** intimate first-person experience that does not reveal unnecessary private facts about children, partners, family members, private citizens, legal cases or health matters.
- **PRIVATE:** identifying or sensitive details whose public value does not outweigh privacy or safety risk; these remain outside the public site.

The rule is: **tell Igor's responsibility, choices and experience; do not use another person's privacy as narrative raw material.**

## Knowledge Commons model

Each major topic gains a reusable `Knowledge Lens` attached to the life chapter that motivates it.

A Knowledge Lens contains:

- question generated by the lived experience
- relevant schools/frameworks
- what each framework explains well
- what each framework misses or disputes
- current evidence level
- source list
- practical implications
- tools / organizations / learning resources
- share / cite / reuse metadata

Knowledge Lenses are editorially independent from Igor's interpretation. They may agree, partially agree, or disagree with his framing.

## Inter-site knowledge architecture

7YA becomes the canonical narrative and public-learning hub, while related sites/projects can consume or link to reusable knowledge objects.

### Stable topic URLs

Every Knowledge Lens receives a stable, human-readable URL and canonical metadata.

Examples:

- `/knowledge/belonging`
- `/knowledge/youth-opportunity`
- `/knowledge/digital-parenting`
- `/knowledge/public-trust`
- `/knowledge/virality-and-impact`

If current routing constraints require hash/relative navigation in AppDeploy, implementation must preserve equivalent stable route semantics within the deployed architecture.

### Cross-site linking

- 7YA life chapters link to relevant Knowledge Lenses.
- StartOn pages link back to the youth-opportunity, belonging, mentoring and digital-inclusion lenses.
- Research pages link to both Igor's original frameworks and established external literature.
- Podcast/interview records link to the Knowledge Lenses they illuminate.
- Viral-post records link to the themes and evidence questions they raise.

No link may imply an institutional endorsement that the source does not establish.

### Reusable public objects

Each Knowledge Lens should expose:

- concise summary
- citation-ready title and description
- source list
- share image / Open Graph metadata where appropriate
- `schema.org` / JSON-LD metadata
- RSS or feed discoverability for new/updated knowledge pages
- printable / teaching-friendly text view where feasible
- compact `Take this forward` resource block

## Home integration

The Knowledge Commons must not become another dashboard before the human story.

Approved first-visit hierarchy remains life-first.

Recommended home rhythm:

1. `LifeFirstHero` — first-person human entry.
2. `PersonalChronology` — deep first-person life chapters.
3. selected post / interview / podcast / public-echo inserts inside the relevant life chapters.
4. `Knowledge Commons` entry points attached contextually to those chapters.
5. `InfluenceUniverse` / public echo.
6. public action, StartOn, research, creation and current-life sections.
7. deep archive and source universe.

The Knowledge Commons is therefore **contextual and distributed**, not one giant theory section placed before the life.

## Chapter integration pattern

Each major life chapter gains three optional expanders below the personal narrative:

### `Hear / Watch this chapter`

Relevant interview, podcast, video, article, song or broadcast from the same life period.

### `See how it travelled`

Originating post or media object → redistribution → audience reaction → publisher/media pickup → later continuation where evidence exists.

### `Learn from this`

Knowledge Lens with schools of thought, evidence, disagreement, practical tools and reusable resources.

All three are subordinate to the first-person story and should not interrupt reading unless opened or editorially featured.

## Content-source rules

### Personal narrative

May use:

- user-approved first-person memory
- verified public biography
- owner archive
- prior canon
- interviews and public statements

When memory and documentary evidence differ, the site should distinguish memory from documented fact rather than silently reconciling them.

### Public influence

Use the 7YA Influence Corpus Canon and audited influence workbooks as the inventory base.

Every recoverable public manifestation may be indexed when provenance is defensible:

- originals
- comments-as-content
- shares / reposts / quote-posts
- screenshots / embeds / reuploads / remixes
- publisher copies
- media reproduction
- reactions and comments on redistributed copies

Metrics remain local to their source object unless a documented methodology justifies aggregation.

### Knowledge / research

Prefer primary research, official institutional material and established academic literature.

Each claim should expose enough provenance to distinguish:

- established evidence
- plausible interpretation
- contested literature
- Igor's own hypothesis/framework
- open question

## Editorial disagreement model

Every Knowledge Lens may contain a compact `Where the schools disagree` module.

The purpose is not artificial balance. It is to show meaningful differences in causal explanation, evidence and recommended intervention.

Examples:

- labeling vs. individual-risk models
- structural opportunity vs. behavioral explanation
- punitive enforcement vs. prevention / procedural-justice approaches
- parental restriction vs. guided digital autonomy
- virality metrics vs. substantive public impact

## Practical-action model

Every Knowledge Lens ends with `What can I do now?` and may include:

- one immediate action
- one deeper learning path
- one organization / public service / community resource where relevant
- one reflection or diagnostic exercise
- one way to contribute or help another person
- one way to continue into StartOn / 7YA / external public resources without turning the site into a sales funnel

## Multilingual behavior

Hebrew, English and Russian remain first-class.

- Personal first-person copy is localized, not machine-literal where that would damage voice.
- Academic terminology preserves accepted discipline-specific vocabulary in each language.
- Source titles may remain in their original language with localized explanations.
- No language version may silently make a stronger claim than another.

## Visual system

The visual rule remains: **real life first, evidence second, illustration only when clearly contextual.**

- No collages.
- Real photographs, original posts, broadcast frames and interview imagery dominate.
- Generated/contextual illustration is allowed only when it does not impersonate documentary evidence.
- Knowledge modules use editorial diagrams, typographic comparison and source cards rather than generic stock photography.
- Viral posts appear as content objects in their chronological context, not as a detached social-media wall.
- Interviews and podcasts use recognizable source frames, covers or episode imagery where available.

## Component architecture

Recommended new focused units:

- `KnowledgeLens` — renders one topic lens.
- `KnowledgeLensCard` — compact contextual entry from a life chapter.
- `SchoolComparison` — multiple frameworks with agreement/disagreement.
- `EvidenceStrength` — established / mixed / emerging / Igor-framework / open-question state.
- `TakeItForward` — resources, tools, organizations and next actions.
- `CrossSiteLinks` — canonical related-project links with relationship-safe labels.
- `StoryMediaInsert` — interview/post/podcast/music object embedded in life chronology.
- `EchoTrail` — verified propagation path for a public content object.

Existing archive, source, locale, StartOn, research, music and influence components should be reused rather than duplicated.

## Data model

A minimal Knowledge Lens object should contain:

```ts
type KnowledgeLens = {
  id: string;
  slug: string;
  title: Local;
  question: Local;
  lifeChapterIds: string[];
  schools: Array<{
    id: string;
    name: Local;
    explanation: Local;
    strengths: Local;
    limitations: Local;
  }>;
  evidenceState: 'established' | 'mixed' | 'emerging' | 'igor-framework' | 'open-question';
  sources: Array<{
    title: string;
    url: string;
    kind: 'paper' | 'official' | 'book' | 'dataset' | 'media' | '7ya-source';
  }>;
  actions: Array<{
    label: Local;
    description: Local;
    href?: string;
  }>;
  related: string[];
};
```

The implementation may adapt naming to existing repository conventions but must preserve these semantic boundaries.

## Error and fallback rules

- Missing research source: omit the claim or mark it as an open question.
- Broken external source: retain title/provenance and show source-unavailable state; do not fabricate substitute content.
- Missing visual: use a designed contextual source card, not unrelated stock.
- Unknown metric: remain unknown; never coerce to zero.
- Disputed metric: display as disputed or quarantined, not canonical.
- Knowledge Lens with insufficient evidence: show `emerging` or `open-question`, not authoritative language.
- Cross-site relationship without verified formal status: use neutral related-project language only.

## SEO and interoperability

Public knowledge pages should support:

- canonical URL
- localized title / description
- Open Graph / social metadata
- Article / Person / CreativeWork / Dataset / PodcastEpisode / VideoObject / ScholarlyArticle schema where semantically accurate
- Breadcrumb / related-topic metadata
- crawlable text
- RSS/feed discovery for new knowledge entries or updates

Structured data must describe what the object actually is; no academic or organizational status inflation.

## Acceptance criteria

The release passes only when all are true:

1. The first screen is still unmistakably Igor's personal first-person story, not a knowledge portal.
2. Personal chronology contains contextual paths into interviews, podcasts, posts and knowledge without becoming a grid dashboard.
3. At least three life chapters demonstrate the full pattern: lived story → media/public expression → public echo → Knowledge Lens → practical action.
4. The first release includes at least five Knowledge Lenses spanning youth/belonging, institutions, public influence, StartOn/opportunity and research/meaning.
5. At least one Knowledge Lens visibly presents meaningful disagreement between schools of thought.
6. Each Knowledge Lens distinguishes evidence state and source provenance.
7. Igor's own frameworks are visibly labeled as his frameworks rather than academic consensus.
8. Cross-site links connect 7YA and relevant StartOn/research resources without implying unsupported endorsement.
9. Viral posts are shown in chronological narrative context and retain source-local metrics.
10. Interviews/podcasts are contextually attached to relevant life chapters.
11. Hebrew, English and Russian render correctly and preserve equivalent claim strength.
12. No collage, generic stock-wall or fabricated historical image is presented as evidence.
13. Mobile experience keeps one dominant narrative/media object at a time and avoids horizontal overflow.
14. Existing evidence/source routes, relationship semantics, research objects, music and archive remain intact.
15. Production QA passes on desktop and mobile before publication.

## Initial implementation slice

The first release should be intentionally narrow enough to validate the model.

Implement five Knowledge Lenses first:

1. **Belonging, migration and youth risk** — attached to childhood / immigration.
2. **Institutions, trust and prevention** — attached to service / policing.
3. **Youth opportunity, belonging and digital inclusion** — attached to StartOn.
4. **Virality, framing and public impact** — attached to public voice / viral content.
5. **Research, meaning and original frameworks** — attached to research / spirituality.

For each slice, include one contextual media insert and one practical `Take it forward` block.

Do not attempt to model every possible topic in the first release.

## Non-goals

- Do not build a generic encyclopedia.
- Do not replace external scholarship with AI summaries lacking sources.
- Do not claim that Igor's biography proves a social-science theory.
- Do not expose private family or child information to make a chapter feel deeper.
- Do not aggregate all social metrics into an inflated total reach.
- Do not redesign the entire deep archive in this slice.
- Do not create new institutional partnership claims.

## Success definition

A visitor should be able to move from:

> "I understand what Igor lived."

into:

> "I can hear and see how he expressed it publicly."

then:

> "I understand several serious ways to interpret the issue, including disagreement."

and finally:

> "I have something useful to learn, share or do because I encountered this story."

The final product is therefore **a living autobiography that gives its accumulated knowledge away.**
