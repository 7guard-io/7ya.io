# 7YA Intellectual Research Spine Design

## Decision

Approved positioning: **Igor Vepretski is presented first as an independent interdisciplinary researcher, creator and public-systems builder whose biography, service, media work, technology practice and social initiatives function as a living field of inquiry.**

The site must stop framing research as a side lane or one content category among many. Research becomes the explanatory spine that connects lived experience, public observation, theory, evidence, creation and implementation.

## Canonical positioning

Primary English descriptor:

> **Independent Interdisciplinary Researcher · Creator · Public Systems Builder**

Primary Hebrew descriptor:

> **חוקר רב־תחומי עצמאי · יוצר · בונה מערכות ציבוריות**

Primary Russian descriptor:

> **Независимый междисциплинарный исследователь · автор · создатель общественных систем**

Core framing sentence:

> **Life is the laboratory. Research is the method for understanding it. Systems are the attempt to turn understanding into action.**

Hebrew:

> **החיים הם המעבדה. המחקר הוא הדרך להבין אותם. המערכות הן הניסיון לתרגם את ההבנה לפעולה.**

This wording must remain claim-safe. Do not imply a university appointment, professorship, institutional research affiliation, peer-reviewed status or externally conferred title unless supported by a source.

## Product objective

Within the first 8–12 seconds on the homepage, a new visitor should understand four things:

1. Igor is not merely a creator or influencer with a biography.
2. His public work is organized around recurring research questions.
3. The research produces named frameworks, manuscripts, preprints, essays or working theories with explicit publication status.
4. StartOn, 7YA and other public systems are applications or experiments that follow from those questions rather than disconnected projects.

The intended visitor takeaway is:

> There is a coherent intellectual system here: lived experience becomes a question; questions become frameworks; frameworks are tested against evidence and public reality; and some become systems, interventions or creative work.

## Research Spine

The site should express the following chain repeatedly and consistently:

**EXPERIENCE → OBSERVATION → QUESTION → FRAMEWORK → EVIDENCE → APPLICATION → OPEN QUESTIONS**

### Experience

The biographical or field condition that generated attention: migration, belonging, policing and public systems, parenting, digital identity, information overload, neighborhood opportunity, security narratives, media distribution or social resilience.

### Observation

The pattern noticed in lived or public reality.

### Question

The research question that makes the observation falsifiable, debatable or investigable.

### Framework

A named concept, hypothesis, manuscript, theoretical model, research program or synthesis.

### Evidence

Sources, literature, public records, datasets, case material, historical evidence or field observations. Evidence must remain distinguishable from autobiography and interpretation.

### Application

A system, intervention, prototype, article, media artifact, public initiative or design experiment created in response.

### Open Questions

Known limitations, unresolved empirical questions, counterarguments and future work. This is a required component, not a weakness to hide.

## Research Map

A canonical research surface should be native to 7YA rather than delegating the entire scholarly identity to Academia.edu.

The research map groups work by questions, not by file type.

### Initial research domains

#### 1. Digital Identity & Algorithmic Mediation

Possible anchor work: **The Resonant Self**.

Question family:

- How do algorithmic systems shape identity formation and perceived social resonance?
- What happens when feedback systems become part of self-construction?
- How should agency be protected in algorithmically mediated environments?

#### 2. Information Resilience & Human Agency

Possible anchor work: **SUPERNOAH**.

Question family:

- How do individuals and communities preserve agency under information overload?
- What forms of filtering, memory, verification and deliberate attention increase resilience?
- How can a digital system support judgment without replacing it?

#### 3. Governance, Incentives & Threat Perception

Possible anchor work: **Strategic Sedation**.

Question family:

- How can incentive structures distort threat perception over time?
- How do mediation, political utility and institutional assumptions produce strategic blindness?
- Which signals should falsify a dominant security narrative?

#### 4. Political Economy of Dependency

Possible anchor work: **Gastrocratia**.

Question family:

- How do food systems become structures of political dependency and governance?
- How does control over basic needs create leverage?
- What institutional conditions reduce dependency-based power?

#### 5. Opportunity, Adversity & Development

Anchor: **Opportunity / Adversity research program**.

Question family:

- How does the density of positive developmental opportunities differ across distressed and resource-rich environments?
- Which combinations of space, adults, tools, peer networks and creative agency change trajectories?
- What can StartOn test empirically?

#### 6. Public Memory, Evidence & Digital Governance

Anchor: **7YA / Evidence Oracle / public memory work**.

Question family:

- What should count as evidence in a personal public archive?
- How can provenance and correction be preserved without turning a human life into a database abstraction?
- How can AI retrieve and explain a public record without impersonation or unsupported claims?

## Research Object contract

Every major research work should render as a structured Research Object with the following fields:

1. **Title**
2. **Domain**
3. **Research question**
4. **Core thesis / proposition**
5. **Framework or model**
6. **Method or evidence basis**
7. **Publication status**
8. **Version / date**
9. **Key propositions**
10. **Limitations / counterarguments**
11. **Related field application**
12. **Primary file or source**
13. **Citation format**
14. **Related public writing / media**
15. **Open questions / future work**

The UI should visually separate these fields rather than flattening them into promotional cards.

## Publication-status taxonomy

Use explicit labels. At minimum:

- **PEER REVIEWED** — only with direct evidence.
- **PREPRINT** — publicly released manuscript not peer reviewed.
- **WORKING PAPER** — active research paper not presented as final.
- **CONCEPTUAL PAPER** — theory-building work without empirical validation.
- **MONOGRAPH / BOOK** — long-form authored work; peer review not implied.
- **ESSAY / PUBLIC THINKING** — authored public argument or synthesis.
- **RESEARCH PROGRAM** — structured agenda with questions, hypotheses or future empirical work.
- **FIELD PILOT** — real-world implementation or test.
- **PROTOTYPE / DESIGN** — system or method under development.

Never convert `Academia.edu indexed`, `uploaded`, `public PDF`, `author profile` or `download count` into `peer reviewed`, `published in a journal`, `academic appointment` or `institutional authority`.

## Relationship between biography and scholarship

The biography is not presented as proof of a theory.

Instead:

**biography provides the origin of questions; evidence and argument provide the basis of claims.**

Examples:

- neighborhood adversity may explain why opportunity became a research question, but it does not by itself prove the opportunity hypothesis;
- public/security service may motivate research into institutions and threat perception, but institutional claims require sources and analysis;
- creator experience may generate hypotheses about algorithmic identity, but research claims must remain distinguishable from personal observation.

This distinction should be visible in both copy and information architecture.

## Relationship between research and StartOn

StartOn should be framed as a **field application and potential empirical environment**, not as automatic proof that the research is correct.

The StartOn chapter should expose explicit research links such as:

- opportunity density;
- belonging and adult support;
- tool access;
- creative agency;
- social capital;
- measurable participation and retention;
- privacy-safe outcome measurement.

Where a result has not been measured, the site must say `hypothesis`, `design`, `pilot` or `measurement pending` rather than `impact proven`.

## Relationship between research and 7YA

7YA becomes the system layer that operationalizes research questions about:

- public memory;
- provenance;
- evidence status;
- correction;
- AI retrieval;
- identity continuity;
- personal agency;
- privacy-by-default;
- creator ownership.

The platform must not become the hero. It remains a research-informed public system serving the human and the mission.

## Visual language

The research layer should feel like **editorial intelligence**, not university bureaucracy.

Use:

- strong portrait photography;
- generous white space or restrained dark editorial surfaces;
- diagrams only when they explain a model;
- visible source/status metadata;
- typographic hierarchy inspired by serious long-form publishing;
- marginalia-like metadata for method, version and status;
- individual research objects, never collage compositions;
- restrained motion showing relationships or progression rather than decorative animation.

Avoid:

- fake journal covers;
- graduation imagery;
- libraries used as authority wallpaper;
- laboratory stock photography unrelated to the work;
- citation walls with no explanatory hierarchy;
- inflated labels such as `world-leading`, `renowned scholar` or `academic authority`.

Prefer a structured content contract for research objects rather than hardcoding promotional copy across components.

Recommended conceptual schema:

The research UI should consume this data contract so status wording and source links remain consistent across homepage, research map, entity page, museum, SEO and AI retrieval surfaces.

## Navigation

Research receives a first-class navigation destination.

Preferred label:

- HE: **מחקר ורעיונות**
- EN: **Research & Ideas**
- RU: **Исследования и идеи**

The destination should be native, e.g. `/research/`, with Academia.edu and other repositories treated as external source or distribution surfaces.

## Error and integrity handling

If a research object lacks a source, publication file or required status metadata:

- keep it unpublished from the canonical research map, or
- mark it clearly as `SOURCE PENDING` / `DRAFT` where owner-approved.

Broken external source links should not silently downgrade the object to an unsupported claim. They should surface a recoverable source-state error for maintenance.

If publication status is ambiguous, choose the more conservative label.

## Testing and acceptance criteria

### Identity

- A first-time visitor can identify Igor as an independent interdisciplinary researcher, creator and public-systems builder in the first viewport.
- Research is structurally visible before the platform layer and not buried under social-media links.

### Research structure

- A native research destination exists.
- At least the approved major frameworks can render as structured Research Objects.
- Each object visibly exposes research question, thesis, status, evidence/source, limitations and application.

### Claim safety

- No unsupported institutional affiliation, degree, academic rank, peer-review status or citation metric is implied.
- Publication-state labels are conservative and source-backed.

### Narrative coherence

- Biography explains the origin of questions but is not used as proof of theoretical claims.
- StartOn is presented as field application/pilot where appropriate, not automatic validation.
- 7YA is presented as a research-informed system, not the central identity.

### Visual quality

- Desktop and mobile preserve readable long-form hierarchy.
- Research content does not collapse into dense card grids.
- No collage compositions.
- Research objects remain individually inspectable.

### Technical quality

- HE/EN/RU routes remain crawlable.
- Canonical URLs and metadata are correct.
- No horizontal overflow on mobile.
- Internal research links resolve.
- Source links remain explicit.
- Existing evidence and privacy rules remain intact.

## Scope boundaries

This design does **not** authorize:

- fabrication of academic affiliations;
- claims of peer review without evidence;
- automatic publication of private drafts;
- replacement of the evidence system;
- removal of social, cultural, music or public-service identity;
- a broad unrelated redesign of infrastructure, billing, authentication or deployment.

It changes the public intellectual hierarchy and the way existing content is organized.

## Smallest coherent implementation sequence

1. Introduce the canonical descriptor and research framing in the homepage hero.
2. Promote Research & Ideas to first-class navigation.
3. Create the structured research-object data contract.
4. Build the native `/research/` map using conservative publication labels.
5. Replace the current shallow `Ideas` treatment on the homepage with Research Spine content.
6. Link StartOn and 7YA explicitly to the research questions they operationalize.
7. Update museum/entity/SEO surfaces to consume the same research metadata.
8. Run visual, crawlability, claim-safety and mobile QA before production promotion.

## Success definition

7YA succeeds when Igor's public identity no longer reads as a list of roles and outputs. It should read as a coherent intellectual trajectory:

**lived experience → observation → research question → framework → evidence → creation/system → public application → revision.**

The visitor should encounter a human being whose research, creation and public systems belong to one intellectual architecture — without exaggerating academic status or hiding uncertainty.
