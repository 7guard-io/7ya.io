7YA is not a project. It is a movement.

A project has features. A movement has gravity. 7YA must feel like a place people enter, remember, and return to.

Build
Learn
Protect
Lead

The old public layer of 7YA had many useful signals: AI systems, documentation, social links, public influence assets, creator tools, and StartOn impact. But when too many signals appear without a central story, the visitor experiences a system instead of a cause.

The new site direction reverses the order. Igor Vepretski comes first. The mission comes second. The action comes third. The tools come after that.

First identity. Then proof. Then belonging. Then action.

The new public promise

7YA is the home for people who want to build useful systems, strengthen youth opportunity, turn personal story into public responsibility, and use digital culture for something stronger than attention.

The entrance must feel earned

The entry experience should borrow the psychology of a legendary unlock without copying any game. A visitor should feel a clean ritual: the screen focuses, the 7 appears, the member pass glows, and the message becomes clear. You are not browsing. You are entering.

The content must become searchable

That is why the Knowledge Stream matters. Articles give the movement memory. They help search engines, journalists, collaborators, and supporters understand who Igor is, why 7YA exists, and where to go next.

The discipline

No fake metrics. No empty hype. No clutter pretending to be depth. Every section must answer one of four questions: who is Igor, why does 7YA exist, why now, and what should I do next?
