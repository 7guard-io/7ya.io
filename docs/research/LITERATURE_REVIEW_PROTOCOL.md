# Opportunity Ecology — Literature Review Protocol

Status: DRAFT PROTOCOL
Date: 2026-08-10
Branch: `research/opportunity-ecology-foundation-20260810`

## 1. Review objective

Determine whether the proposed Opportunity Ecology framework adds explanatory or measurement value beyond existing theory, and identify the strongest empirical evidence relevant to inequality in developmental opportunities and opportunity conversion.

The review must answer five questions:

1. How has "opportunity" been defined and operationalized across sociology, economics, developmental psychology, criminology, education and public health?
2. Which established constructs most closely overlap with Opportunity Ecology, Developmental Opportunity Density, Opportunity Conversion and Opportunity Multiplication?
3. What evidence supports causal or longitudinal effects of neighborhood, relationships, institutions, technology access and structured activities on youth outcomes?
4. Which mechanisms are modifiable by an intervention such as StartOn or a digital system such as 7YA?
5. What measurement gap, if any, remains after accounting for existing constructs?

## 2. Review type

Phase 1: broad scoping review and conceptual synthesis.

Phase 2: focused systematic reviews/meta-analytic synthesis where the evidence base and measurement comparability justify it.

Do not label Phase 1 a systematic review until database coverage, search reproducibility, duplicate handling, screening and reporting requirements are satisfied.

## 6. Eligibility criteria

### Include
Studies or reviews that materially address at least one of:
- availability or accessibility of developmental opportunities;
- neighborhood/context effects on youth development;
- mechanisms linking relationships/networks to mobility or development;
- conversion of resources/exposure into capabilities or outcomes;
- modifiable interventions intended to expand opportunity;
- longitudinal or causal evidence on relevant mechanisms;
- validated measures that could overlap with proposed constructs.

Priority populations:
- children;
- adolescents;
- emerging adults where developmental continuity is relevant;
- disadvantaged, low-income or structurally excluded populations;
- general-population studies when mechanisms are theoretically transferable.

### Exclude or downgrade
- purely inspirational or non-empirical claims presented as evidence;
- studies without sufficient methodological description;
- cross-sectional associations when stronger longitudinal/causal evidence exists for the same claim;
- measures whose construct validity is unclear when used as direct proxies for opportunity;
- intervention reports that report satisfaction only and no mechanism/outcome data;
- sources that conflate correlation with causation.

## 7. Evidence hierarchy

For each claim, record design strength separately from publication prestige.

Suggested design categories:

A — randomized/credible natural experiment or strong quasi-experimental causal design;
B — prospective longitudinal study with appropriate confounding control;
C — validated measurement/psychometric study;
D — systematic review/meta-analysis, with quality assessment of included studies;
E — cross-sectional observational study;
F — qualitative/mechanistic evidence;
G — conceptual/theoretical work;
H — descriptive/administrative data.

A study can be highly valuable in one category without answering causal questions.

## 9. Novelty test

Before claiming any proposed construct is novel, test it against at least these neighboring concepts:

- capability and conversion factors;
- opportunity structure;
- geography of opportunity;
- social capital / bridging capital;
- developmental assets;
- developmental relationships;
- positive youth development;
- ecological systems;
- relational developmental systems;
- cumulative advantage/disadvantage;
- cumulative opportunity;
- social mobility;
- digital capital;
- resilience resources;
- life-course turning points.

A new label is not a contribution. A contribution requires at least one of:

1. a distinct construct with discriminant validity;
2. an integrative model that explains relationships existing models leave fragmented;
3. a measurable mechanism with predictive or causal value;
4. a validated instrument or data architecture that enables previously difficult testing;
5. a falsifiable prediction not already entailed by neighboring theories.

## 10. Bias controls

The research team must actively search for evidence that contradicts the preferred StartOn/7YA narrative.

Required counter-searches:
- null effects of neighborhood interventions;
- harmful or ineffective mentoring;
- digital interventions with no durable benefit;
- intervention implementation failure;
- adverse effects of forced positive reframing;
- critiques of post-traumatic growth measurement;
- evidence that individual-level programs cannot compensate for structural deprivation;
- evidence of selection bias in successful youth programs.

Maintain a `Disconfirming Evidence` column in the evidence matrix.

## 14. Stop conditions

Do not proceed to a large efficacy trial if:
- the construct cannot be distinguished from existing validated concepts;
- measurement reliability/validity is inadequate;
- the intervention dosage is unstable or undefined;
- outcome collection cannot protect minors' privacy;
- there is no credible comparator/counterfactual;
- implementation fidelity cannot be measured.

In those cases, redesign the construct, measure or intervention first.

## 15. First synthesis question

The initial synthesis will test this proposition:

> Developmental inequality can be modeled not only as a difference in resources and outcomes, but as a difference in the longitudinal opportunity set surrounding a young person and in the probability that available opportunities are recognized, accessed and converted into durable capabilities and further opportunities.

This proposition remains a hypothesis until the literature crosswalk and empirical work establish its incremental value.
