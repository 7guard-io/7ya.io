# Opportunity Ecology Research Program — Foundational Charter

Status: RESEARCH / DESIGN — not an efficacy claim
Date: 2026-08-10
Owner: Igor Vepretski / 7YA research track
Canonical branch: `research/opportunity-ecology-foundation-20260810`

## 1. Purpose

Build a rigorous, falsifiable research program around a central question:

> To what extent are positive developmental opportunities unequally distributed, recognized and converted across childhood and adolescence, especially in disadvantaged neighborhoods — and can deliberately designed physical and digital opportunity infrastructures measurably improve developmental trajectories?

The program must be able to stand independently of StartOn and 7YA. Its purpose is not to prove that either project works. Its purpose is to create a strong theoretical and empirical test under which they may succeed, fail or require redesign.

## 2. Core theoretical proposition

Inequality is not only inequality in resources or outcomes. It may also be inequality in the quantity, quality, accessibility, timing, diversity, recognition and convertibility of positive developmental opportunities.

Working definition:

> A positive developmental opportunity is an accessible encounter with a person, institution, resource, knowledge domain, technology, role, activity or pathway that has a realistic probability of expanding a person's future capabilities, choices, relationships or constructive agency.

Opportunity pathway:

`Existence -> Accessibility -> Recognition -> Engagement -> Conversion -> Capability -> New Opportunity`

The final arrow is central: some opportunities may have multiplicative effects by generating access to further opportunities.

## 3. Working constructs

These names are provisional until systematic literature review establishes novelty, overlap and discriminant validity.

### Opportunity Ecology
The dynamic environment of opportunities, constraints, relationships and conversion mechanisms surrounding a person across time and place.

### Developmental Opportunity Density (DOD)
A candidate construct for the density of meaningful positive developmental opportunities available to a person within a defined time-window and ecological context.

Possible dimensions:
- availability;
- physical/digital accessibility;
- affordability;
- developmental timing;
- quality;
- diversity;
- safety;
- social bridge value;
- recognizability;
- conversion probability;
- continuity;
- opportunity multiplication potential.

### Opportunity Conversion
The process by which exposure becomes participation, learning, capability, social connection, credential, project, employment, education, contribution or another durable outcome.

### Opportunity Multiplication
The degree to which one converted opportunity creates access to additional opportunities.

### Negative Opportunity Surplus
A hypothesis that some disadvantaged environments may combine scarcity of high-return positive opportunities with high accessibility and short-delay rewards from risky or harmful pathways.

This must be investigated carefully and must not stigmatize neighborhoods or residents.

## 4. GROW transformation model

GROW is a product and explanatory model, not yet a validated psychological instrument.

- G — Gather: identify life material as it is — adversity, failures, strengths, relationships, skills, resources and constraints.
- R — Reframe: develop an accurate interpretation without denying harm, romanticizing suffering or assigning false meaning.
- O — Opportunity: identify realistic pathways through which insight, capacity or support can become a constructive opportunity.
- W — Work / Grow: sustained action, feedback and contribution that can produce measurable development.

Human-development sequence under investigation:

`Adversity -> Processing -> Meaning/Integration -> Capability -> Opportunity -> Action -> Growth -> Contribution -> More Opportunity`

Important safeguard: adversity is not assumed to cause growth. Harm may remain harm. Growth, resilience and meaning-making are contingent processes affected by individual, relational and structural factors.

## 6. StartOn hypothesis

Current program hypothesis — not a verified outcome claim:

> StartOn may function as physical opportunity infrastructure by concentrating safe technology access, learning, creation, mentors, community, belonging and bridging relationships in environments where such opportunities may otherwise be less accessible.

Primary mechanism candidates:

`Safe place -> belonging -> repeated attendance -> developmental relationships -> technology exposure -> skill/agency -> projects -> bridging social capital -> education/work pathways -> contribution`

Each arrow must become separately measurable and falsifiable.

## 7. 7YA hypothesis

7YA should be studied and designed as digital opportunity infrastructure, not as a generic chatbot and not as an automated authority.

Target function:

`Listen -> Understand -> Map -> Prioritize -> Connect -> Create -> Act -> Reflect -> Grow`

The system may support:
- longitudinal goal and progress memory with explicit user control;
- opportunity discovery and matching;
- learning pathway generation;
- project creation tools;
- network and mentor navigation;
- reflection and meaning-making support;
- evidence-bound recommendations;
- progress measurement;
- conversion from intention to concrete next action.

The AI must not impersonate Igor, replace professional care, make unsupported deterministic life judgments, or convert spiritual claims into scientific claims.

## 9. Research program

### Study A — Conceptual and systematic review
Goals:
- map existing constructs and theories;
- prevent false novelty claims;
- identify measurement gaps;
- create a formal taxonomy of developmental opportunity.

Required literatures include:
- neighborhood effects;
- social mobility;
- social capital and economic connectedness;
- developmental relationships;
- positive youth development;
- resilience and multisystemic resilience;
- adverse childhood experiences;
- post-traumatic growth and its measurement critiques;
- meaning-making;
- capability approach;
- life-course development;
- ecological systems / relational developmental systems;
- skill formation and dynamic complementarity;
- youth mentoring;
- digital inequality and digital capital;
- opportunity structures;
- desistance and turning points;
- implementation science;
- causal program evaluation.

### Study B — Measurement development
Develop and validate an Opportunity Ecology instrument or measurement system.

Candidate data sources:
- youth self-report;
- caregiver report where appropriate;
- mentor/staff observation;
- neighborhood administrative indicators;
- service availability;
- geospatial accessibility;
- network structure;
- digital access/use;
- event-level opportunity logs;
- longitudinal project and skill records.

Psychometric requirements:
- content validity;
- construct validity;
- convergent/discriminant validity;
- reliability;
- measurement invariance where relevant;
- predictive validity;
- sensitivity to change;
- burden and usability testing.

### Study C — Opportunity mapping in Israel
Map variation in developmental opportunity across selected neighborhoods without reducing communities to deficit labels.

Compare opportunity access with:
- socioeconomic indicators;
- educational access;
- youth services;
- digital infrastructure;
- transport/accessibility;
- mentor/network exposure;
- youth-reported opportunity recognition and conversion.

### Study D — StartOn evaluation
Preferred design depends on rollout constraints.

Candidate hierarchy:
1. randomized or randomized-waitlist design where feasible and ethical;
2. stepped-wedge rollout;
3. regression discontinuity if eligibility thresholds exist;
4. matched longitudinal comparison;
5. difference-in-differences when rollout timing creates a credible comparison;
6. interrupted time-series for specific outcomes.

Avoid relying solely on pre/post satisfaction surveys.

Measure both mechanisms and outcomes.

### Study E — 7YA digital opportunity intervention
Test whether 7YA improves conversion, not merely engagement.

Potential outcomes:
- opportunity identification quality;
- task completion;
- learning persistence;
- project completion;
- mentor/contact activation;
- application/submission completion;
- skill acquisition;
- perceived agency;
- belonging and future orientation;
- verified movement toward education/work/community goals.

## 10. Evidence standard

Every important statement must be classified as one of:

- VERIFIED — supported by strong direct evidence;
- DOCUMENTED — supported by a dated record or source, but not necessarily causal;
- INFERRED — reasoned interpretation;
- HYPOTHESIS — testable proposition;
- DESIGN — proposed intervention or product behavior;
- SELF-ATTESTED — biographical statement without independent verification attached;
- SOURCE PENDING — not ready for public factual presentation.

No impact claim about StartOn or 7YA should be promoted from DESIGN/HYPOTHESIS to VERIFIED without appropriate evidence.

## 11. Ethical design principles

- Youth are participants and agents, not research objects or deficit categories.
- No forced disclosure of trauma.
- No requirement to produce a positive meaning from suffering.
- Privacy by default and data minimization.
- Minors' identifying data excluded from public evidence surfaces.
- Human escalation paths for safeguarding concerns.
- Avoid algorithmic ranking that labels a young person as doomed, dangerous or low-potential.
- Recommendations should increase choice rather than manipulate behavior.
- Study protocols involving minors require appropriate ethics review, consent/assent and safeguarding procedures.

## 12. Product implications for 7YA

7YA should eventually maintain, under explicit user control, an `Opportunity Map` containing:
- current goals;
- constraints;
- strengths;
- skills;
- relationships/resources;
- available opportunities;
- opportunity stage: discovered / eligible / contacted / active / converted / closed;
- next action;
- evidence/source;
- user feedback;
- longitudinal outcomes.

The product's north-star metric should not be message count or time-on-chat.

Candidate north-star concept:

> Verified Opportunity Conversion: the proportion and quality of user-relevant opportunities that progress from discovery to a meaningful, user-chosen action or durable capability.

This requires further validation and must not be gamed by pushing users toward unnecessary actions.

## 15. Success criterion

The project succeeds scientifically only if an independent researcher can:

1. understand exactly what is being claimed;
2. distinguish theory, narrative, design and evidence;
3. reproduce the measurement and analysis logic;
4. identify plausible alternative explanations;
5. test the claims using data;
6. obtain a negative result without the system redefining failure as success.

The ultimate practical objective is not to claim that every hardship becomes growth. It is to increase the probability that people, especially those with fewer structural opportunities, can access, recognize and convert constructive opportunities — and eventually create opportunities for others.
