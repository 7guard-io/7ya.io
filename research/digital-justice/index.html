Digital
Justice.

A practical rights-and-evidence framework for a world where digital records and automated systems can affect a person’s opportunities, identity and choices.

NORTH STAR

No person should be powerless against a digital record or an automated decision about their own life.

01 / THE RIGHTS

Seven rights.
One human position.

The protocol starts with the person. Technology may assist a decision, record a claim or organize evidence; it should not erase the ability to understand, challenge and correct what affects you.

01

Right to Know

Know when a consequential digital record or automated process is being used and what role it plays.

02

Right to Source

See where consequential information came from, its date, status and relevant provenance.

03

Right to Explanation

Receive a useful explanation of the factors, rules or evidence that materially shaped an outcome.

04

Right to Correct

Contest inaccurate, outdated or misleading records and submit better evidence.

05

Right to Challenge

Reach a real review path when a system or record has meaningful consequences.

06

Right to Evidence

Preserve a reviewable record of relevant inputs, sources, changes and outcomes without unnecessary exposure of private data.

07

Right to Remedy

A successful challenge should lead to an actionable correction, reversal, annotation or other appropriate remedy.

02 / REFERENCE FLOW

Rights become real
through process.

A right that cannot be exercised is only a statement. The reference flow turns the protocol into an auditable sequence.

01

Notice

Tell the person what system or record matters.

02

Capture

Preserve the relevant state, date and context.

03

Provenance

Attach source, origin and change history where available.

04

AI Receipt

Create a compact receipt for material AI-assisted actions and outputs.

05

Challenge

Provide a clear path to dispute, add evidence and request review.

06

Remedy

Record the correction or outcome and preserve the audit trail.

03 / PILOT

Start with cases.
Not slogans.

Version 0.1 is intended to be tested against real, consented cases before anyone treats it as a standard.

90-day pilot

  • Recruit a small set of consented cases where a digital record or automated process is disputed.
  • Preserve evidence and provenance with minimum necessary data.
  • Generate a human-readable case receipt.
  • Run a challenge-and-review path.
  • Document whether a correction or remedy was achieved.

What to measure

  • Can the affected person understand what happened?
  • Can an independent reviewer reconstruct the evidence chain?
  • How long does a challenge take?
  • Was inaccurate information corrected or meaningfully annotated?
  • What private data did the process avoid collecting?
  • Which steps fail across institutions or jurisdictions?
STATUS / LIMITS

Digital Justice Protocol v0.1 is an independent public working draft. It is not a law, certification, compliance claim, legal advice or adopted international standard. The point of publishing early is to make the assumptions visible, invite criticism and test the framework against evidence before stronger claims are made.

Help test
